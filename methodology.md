# Methodology

## Research design

The project used a quantitative, cross-sectional survey design. Participants were undergraduate business students who had visited a shopping mall at least once. Responses were collected face to face using 5-point Likert-scale statements.

## Sample

- Final response count: **151**
- Sampling approach: quota sampling followed by convenience selection
- Fieldwork window: approximately 1.5 months
- Analysis software: IBM SPSS Statistics

## Construct groups

- Brand preference
- Usage habits
- Internal and external benefits, including accessibility and experience
- Marketing communication
- Socio-demographic characteristics
- Purchase behavior

## Statistical test map

| Method | Purpose in the study |
|---|---|
| Cronbach's alpha | Assessed scale reliability |
| Kolmogorov-Smirnov | Examined distributional assumptions |
| Chi-square | Tested association between categorical responses |
| Exploratory factor analysis | Evaluated dimensional structure and construct validity |
| Kruskal-Wallis | Compared more than two independent groups |
| Mann-Whitney U | Compared two independent groups |

## Selected statistical evidence

- Brand-preference reliability: **Cronbach's alpha = 0.795**
- Overall factor-analysis adequacy: **KMO = 0.821**
- Ten factors explained **66.513%** of total variance
- Similar-brand perception × branch: **χ²(12) = 24.908, p = .015**
- Floor-plan usefulness × gender: **χ²(4) = 9.893, p = .042**

## Interpretation boundary

Because the project used a student-only, non-probability sample and self-reported responses, the results should not be generalized to the full consumer population or treated as causal estimates.
