# Shopping Mall Consumer Behavior Analysis

Quantitative market-research case study examining how brand choice, accessibility, shopping habits, experience factors and marketing communication relate to consumer purchase behavior in shopping malls.

![Project cover](assets/project-cover.png)

> **Portfolio note:** This was completed as an academic team project at Istanbul University. This repository presents a privacy-safe portfolio edition and does not claim sole authorship of the original research.

## Project snapshot

- **Sample:** 151 undergraduate business students who had visited a shopping mall at least once
- **Research design:** Face-to-face survey with 5-point Likert-scale items
- **Sampling:** Quota sampling followed by convenience selection
- **Analysis environment:** IBM SPSS Statistics
- **Methods:** Cronbach's alpha, Kolmogorov-Smirnov, Chi-square, exploratory factor analysis, Kruskal-Wallis and Mann-Whitney U

## Main question

How do brand preference, usage habits, accessibility, experience factors, marketing communication and socio-demographic characteristics relate to shopping-mall purchase behavior?

## Key findings

- Accessibility was the strongest mall-choice signal; mean scores ranged from **4.12 to 4.25** across gender groups.
- The presence of preferred brands strongly supported revisit intention, with scores between **4.22 and 4.38**.
- Influencer collaborations produced the weakest response scores, ranging from **2.34 to 2.73**.
- The overall factor-analysis suitability was strong (**KMO = 0.821**), and ten factors explained **66.513%** of total variance.
- Visit frequency and monthly mall spending produced more significant group differences than monthly income.
- Most survey items did not differ by gender; significant differences were concentrated in service quality, safety, loyalty, family-friendly features and social responsibility.

## Business recommendations

1. Segment campaigns by lifestyle, motivation and expected benefits rather than income alone.
2. Make accessibility and ease of transport central to mall positioning.
3. Use high-demand brands as visible traffic anchors and improve wayfinding.
4. Build stronger social and experiential reasons to spend time at the mall.
5. Reassess broad influencer spending; prioritize credible niche creators and measurable outcomes.

## Limitations

The sample consists only of university students and was selected using non-probability methods. Results should therefore be interpreted as relationships within this sample, not as population-wide causal effects.
